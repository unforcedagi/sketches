why this is interesting — philosophy · technique · aesthetics

Philosophy — brightness is coincidence. Nothing yellow lives in the water. A ray of sunlight hits the surface, bends, and strikes the floor; its neighbour does the same, a little differently, because the surface under it is a little different. Most of the floor is visited by about one family of rays. Some tiles are visited by many families at once, and those tiles light up. The picture is not a picture of waves. It is a picture of independent paths agreeing. Aftershock, in this collection, draws where an event happened. This draws where many events that never met still land on the same stone.

Technique — a cheap lens, then its envelope. The surface is a discrete wave equation: height and velocity, a four-neighbour Laplacian, a damping sponge at the rim so the square does not ring like a drum. Each tick, every interior cell takes the local slope, builds a normal, and refracts a downward incident ray from air into water. The ray is followed to a plane depth cells below. Intensity is not a photon count. It is 1 / area of the image of a surface triangle on that plane — the inverse Jacobian of the floor map, which is the definition of a caustic. A Gaussian bump is a weak convex lens; the fold of that lens is a ring. Rain is a chorus of those lenses, and the bright X where two rings cross is two folds sharing a focal set. Slopes are scaled 2.2× so that pond-like depth-to-amplitude ratios fit on a 240-cell grid without the waves having to be physically tall.

Aesthetics — draw the agreement, not the pond. The default image is only the floor. Mixing a dim height-shade into that image would repeat a mistake: a second reading that does not actually read, just lifts the black. Surface is a separate view (the look menu, or hold X) so you can see the cause without contaminating the effect. Log exposure keeps a tight focus and a broad wash in one picture; the meter is the ratio of the brightest tile to the mean, which is how concentrated the agreement currently is. The palettes stay in water — lagoon, noon, dusk — because the phenomenon is sunlight, not a heatmap of a field.
